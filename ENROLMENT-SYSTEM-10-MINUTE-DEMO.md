# Enrolment and Progression Support System

## 10-minute screen-share plan and spoken script

### Purpose and audience

This demonstration is designed for:

- referees supporting an application for promotion from Lecturer B to Lecturer C;
- school and institute leaders considering broader adoption of the system.

The central story is not simply that a website was built. It is that a difficult, risky and heavily manual institutional process was identified, progressively redesigned, and converted into a more student-centred and sustainable system.

The demonstration should show evidence of:

- leadership and personal initiative;
- sustained innovation over several years;
- deep understanding of student and operational needs;
- systems thinking and institutional knowledge capture;
- measurable benefits for students, staff and the school;
- potential value beyond the Bachelor of Information Technology.

---

## Before presenting

Prepare a demonstration student whose record produces a useful but manageable set of alerts. Avoid using a real student’s identifying information in a recorded or widely shared demonstration.

Open these items before screen sharing:

1. The opening slide: **From fragmented enrolment processing to integrated decision support**.
2. One representative Strata or spreadsheet screenshot with identifying information removed.
3. The former Excel Enrolment Assistant.
4. The staff website with demonstration files already available.
5. The student website in a separate browser tab.
6. The final outcomes slide.

Use a large browser font and turn off notifications. Rehearse every interaction and keep backup screenshots in the slides in case a live action fails.

Do not attempt to demonstrate every feature. The aim is to establish the scale of the transformation, demonstrate a few compelling examples, and explain the resulting value.

# Spoken script

## 0:00–0:45 — Introduction

**On screen:** Opening slide titled **From fragmented enrolment processing to integrated decision support**.

> I would like to show you the evolution of a system I initiated to improve enrolment and course-progression support.
>
> This began with a practical problem: making a correct enrolment decision required staff to assemble information from many disconnected sources while a student waited for an answer.
>
> Over several years, I progressively redesigned that process—first through an advanced Excel system and now through separate staff and student web applications.
>
> The result is not simply a software project. It is a decision-support and knowledge-management system intended to produce faster, clearer and safer outcomes for students, staff and the school.

## 0:45–1:45 — The problem

**On screen:** A simple “Before” slide showing Strata, spreadsheets, PDFs, email, SharePoint and staff memory feeding into one enrolment decision.

> Previously, an enrolment officer might need to search Strata, sort an exported history, cross subjects off a PDF course map, compare a separate timetable, and search emails and SharePoint for credit, articulation or exceptional-student information.
>
> At the same time, the officer had to reason about prerequisites, failures, old codes, majors, electives, clashes, alternate-semester subjects, visa considerations and the Capstone sequence.
>
> Much of the operational knowledge was undocumented or held in the memories of a few experienced staff. The problem was not the capability or commitment of those staff; it was a fragmented system that placed an unreasonable burden on capable people.
>
> This created cognitive burden and risks: inconsistent advice, missed information, repeated subjects, incorrect credit decisions and delayed graduation. At its worst, an error could contribute to an international student needing another semester in Australia, with extra fees, living costs and visa pressure.

## 1:45–2:40 — The Excel transformation

**On screen:** The former Excel Enrolment Assistant. Enter or show a demonstration student number, then briefly show the visual progression view and timetable.

> I began addressing this problem on my own initiative approximately eight years ago.
>
> I developed the Excel Enrolment Management System progressively from real cases and enrolment feedback. It became a sophisticated platform and has been a required part of our Bachelor of IT enrolment process for around six years.
>
> For the first time, staff could see progression visually, identify completed and remaining subjects, test enrolment choices and produce a timetable much more quickly.
>
> This was a major improvement, but it remained a large staff-operated workbook ecosystem. Students still depended on an officer to interpret it in meetings and emails, and the growing spreadsheet became difficult to extend and maintain.

## 2:40–3:15 — The next evolution

**On screen:** Staff website landing view, followed briefly by the file-loading area.

> During the past six months, I have led the next stage: translating years of operational knowledge into a browser-based platform.
>
> The staff application processes the required institutional exports locally in the browser for the current session. Student data is not uploaded to or permanently stored by the website. If the platform is adopted more broadly, this local-processing model should continue to be reviewed through formal privacy, security and governance processes.
>
> There is also a separate student-facing version. This separation is important: staff receive the operational intelligence they need, while students receive a safe planning environment without sensitive institutional notes.

## 3:15–5:45 — Staff decision support

**On screen:** A prepared demonstration student in the staff application.

### Show the student history and Course Map

> Here, the student’s history becomes a visual course map. Completed subjects, current study, remaining requirements, majors and electives can be understood at a glance.
>
> The system also handles replacement and old subject codes, so historical records are interpreted against the current course.

### Select a major and subjects

> I can change the proposed major and immediately see the implications. The system recalculates requirements instead of relying on staff memory.
>
> As I select subjects, it checks prerequisites, semester availability, course rules and the student’s remaining structure.

### Show alerts and cautions

> Importantly, it does not merely display data. It actively identifies risk.
>
> It can detect unsatisfied prerequisites, repeat failures, missing credits, excess electives, overloads, clashes, inconsistent records, graduation risks and Capstone problems.
>
> The most serious alerts require acknowledgement before the enrolment process continues. This makes important checks part of the workflow instead of relying on memory.

### Show timetable and future-semester planning

> The selections become a readable timetable with staff, room and clash information. I can then move through future semesters because a valid choice now may create too few options later. This supports progression planning, not just immediate selection.

## 5:45–6:55 — Integrated staff workflow

**On screen:** Briefly show student details, copyable timetable or progression summary, email tools, and a supporting-folder shortcut.

> Enrolment decisions are only part of the work. Staff must communicate decisions, prepare forms, locate documents and contact the correct students.
>
> The platform combines Source and Triage information, notes, communication history and operational status. It produces copyable histories, timetables and progression summaries, assists with email scripts, and opens supporting folders and forms. It also helps reconcile student groups and contact details previously rebuilt from overlapping spreadsheets.
>
> This reduces repetitive searching and writing, but more importantly, it makes the process more consistent and easier for another staff member to understand and continue.

## 6:55–7:55 — Student independence

**On screen:** Student-facing website. Enter a short demonstration history, select a major and try one valid and one invalid choice.

> The student version is a major change from the Excel system.
>
> Students can enter completed subjects, explore majors, test combinations, understand prerequisites, see their timetable and examine future progression.
>
> If a choice is not possible or creates a risk, the system explains why. This gives students clearer information before or during an enrolment discussion.
>
> It supports independence without pretending every case is simple. Students gain ownership, while exceptional situations can still be escalated. This should reduce lengthy meetings and improve the conversations that remain.

## 7:55–8:45 — Comprehensiveness and the work behind it

**On screen:** A single slide grouping capabilities under **Progression**, **Risk**, **Operations**, **Communication**, **Accessibility** and **Privacy**.

> What I have shown is only a small part of the system.
>
> It incorporates course structures, prerequisites, semester and timetable logic, Capstone, repeat-fail and APR processes, credit and articulation, visa workflows, enrolment dates, certificates, student flags, communication tools, accessibility and many edge cases.
>
> That comprehensiveness matters because enrolment failures often occur at the intersections between rules rather than within one simple rule.
>
> I want to emphasise that I initiated and sustained this work myself in response to an operational need. The system represents around eight years of accumulated process improvement, including over five years developing the workbook ecosystem and an intensive recent period building and testing the web platform.
>
> AI accelerated coding, refactoring and testing during the website phase and made the scale of the recent implementation achievable. It did not originate the system. The problem definition, accumulated rules, workflow design, safeguards, acceptance decisions and responsibility for validation remained mine.
>
> I have also started making the system maintainable by others. Changing semester information is being separated from stable application logic into human-readable configuration, supported by validation and documented update procedures. Wider adoption would require formal ownership and governance rather than continuing to depend on one person.

## 8:45–10:00 — Outcomes, leadership and future value

**On screen:** Final illustrated slide showing the Enrolment and Progression Platform bridging effective staff, empowered students and Melbourne Polytechnic outcomes.

> This image summarises the wider purpose of the platform. At the centre, it connects people, information and processes.
>
> On the left, staff gain faster processing, greater consistency, better information and more time for meaningful student support. On the right, students gain clearer pathways, greater independence, better-informed choices and a better enrolment experience.
>
> The capabilities underneath—risk detection, workflow automation, communication management, progression modelling and local data processing—support those human outcomes. Together, they can contribute to institutional efficiency, student satisfaction, retention, reputation and, ultimately, growth. I describe these as intended contributions, not guaranteed results.
>
> Based on operational experience, many ongoing enrolments can now be completed in five to ten minutes. Formal evaluation should next measure time, corrections, staff experience and student feedback.
>
> For me, this work demonstrates leadership: I identified a systemic problem, initiated a solution beyond my normal teaching duties, and sustained its development from an Excel improvement into this broader platform.
>
> It now provides a working solution for the Bachelor of IT and a transferable model for other schools, subject to local consultation, rule mapping, validation and governance.

# How to speak to the final image

Move through the image in this order:

1. Point to the platform in the centre: it connects people, information and processes.
2. Move left to effective staff: reduced workload, faster processing and more time for student support.
3. Move right to empowered students: clearer pathways, informed choices and greater independence.
4. Briefly identify the five supporting capabilities along the bottom.
5. Finish at the institutional outcomes across the top.

Do not claim that the platform alone guarantees growth or retention. Say that better student experience, reduced risk and more efficient processes can **contribute** to those outcomes.

**Final spoken line:**  
> A staff-led response to an operational problem has evolved into a student-centred platform with wider institutional potential—better for staff, better for students and better for Melbourne Polytechnic.
